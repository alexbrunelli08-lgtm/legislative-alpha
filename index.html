How this works

01 — Returns are measured twice

As traded runs from the day a member dealt — their own experience, and a price no one else could get. As disclosed runs from the first close after the STOCK Act filing became public, which is the earliest a reader could have acted. Every ranking here can be switched between the two; the gap between them is the part of the move that happens inside the disclosure blackout.

02 — Benchmarked, then shrunk

A raw return flatters everyone in a bull market, so each buy is measured against the S&P over the same holding period. Members are then ranked on a confidence-adjusted alpha: thin records get pulled toward the group median, so a lucky two-trade streak cannot top the board. A record needs at least three priced buys to be ranked at all.

03 — Tested against luck

Each member gets a t-statistic on one observation per distinct ticker, not per trade — buying the same stock ten times is one idea repeated, and pooling the trades would inflate the result. Names are flagged only at |t| > 2. Holding periods still overlap and all end on the same day, so read that as worth a look, not as a p-value: test forty people and roughly two clear the bar on luck alone.

About this data. Bills from the Congress.gov API; trades from Senate and House STOCK Act disclosures (Senate candidates and House paper filings are excluded). Dollar figures are estimates — filings report a value range per trade and we use the midpoint, so every "$" figure is approximate, and dollar-weighted returns inherit that error. Returns are paper returns on the disclosed side of a position: a purchase is marked to the latest close, and because filings rarely reveal exits, nothing here is a realised P&L. Prices are daily closes, so an intraday fill is not modelled, and no transaction costs, taxes or slippage are deducted — a real follower would earn less than any figure shown. "Stocks that benefit" is a sector mapping, not a prediction. Click any ticker to see every disclosed trade in it and the bills that would affect it. Sector and company lists live in scripts/sectors.json. Refreshes every 6 hours. Not investment advice.
